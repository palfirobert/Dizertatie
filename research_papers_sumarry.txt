1.	Energy-aware resource management in cloud computing considering load balance
The paper, "Energy-Aware Resource Management in Cloud Computing Considering Load Balance," addresses the growing energy consumption and operational costs associated with
cloud computing infrastructure. The authors propose a multi-objective optimization model for resource management that focuses on minimizing the number of active physical servers and
balancing workloads across them. This is achieved by considering both energy efficiency and load balancing simultaneously, which many previous studies overlooked.
To tackle the problem, the authors developed a heuristic-based algorithm called the Greedy- Based Load Balance (GBLB). The algorithm operates in two phases:
1.	High-Utilization Adjustment: Reduces server utilization below a predefined upper threshold by migrating selected virtual machines (VMs).
2.	Low-Utilization Consolidation: Consolidates VMs on under-utilized servers and powers down idle servers to save energy.
The GBLB algorithm is evaluated against three existing methods:
•	Dynamic Consolidation with Migration Control (DCMC),
•	Double-Threshold VM Selection (DTVS),
•	Optimal VM Placement (OVMP).
The evaluations were performed using both synthetic workloads and real-world data (Google cluster-usage traces). Metrics such as the number of active servers, number of migrations, load
balance standard deviation (LB_std), and average CPU utilization were used for comparison. The results demonstrated that GBLB achieved the best load balancing and minimized the number of active servers, albeit with slightly higher VM migration costs compared to DTVS.
The authors emphasize that reducing active servers is prioritized over minimizing migrations due to the significant energy savings achieved. The paper concludes that the proposed GBLB
algorithm provides a practical solution for energy-aware resource management. However, it acknowledges limitations, such as assuming constant VM resource demands, and suggests future research to address variable demands and multi-core server scenarios.
 
2.	Energy-Effcient Scheduling of HPC Applications in Cloud Computing Environments
The paper titled "Energy-Efficient Scheduling of HPC Applications in Cloud Computing Environments" tackles the critical issue of high energy consumption in cloud computing,
particularly for High-Performance Computing (HPC) applications. The study emphasizes the need for energy-efficient solutions to address rising operational costs and environmental
concerns caused by carbon emissions. It introduces a novel approach to scheduling HPC
workloads across geographically distributed data centers, focusing on both economic (profit) and environmental (carbon emissions) sustainability.
A mathematical model is proposed to optimize energy usage by considering factors like energy costs, CO2 emission rates, workload characteristics, and CPU power efficiency. The authors
present meta-scheduling policies, including Dynamic Voltage Scaling (DVS), to dynamically adjust CPU operating frequencies and achieve energy savings. Policies like Greedy Minimum Carbon Emission (GMCE) and Minimum Carbon Emission with Maximum Profit (MCE-MP) are designed to either minimize carbon emissions or maximize profits, depending on the cloud provider's objectives.
The evaluation uses real-world HPC workload traces and demonstrates that the proposed policies can save up to 30% energy compared to profit-based methods. By leveraging the heterogeneity of data centers (e.g., varying energy costs and emission rates across locations), the model ensures efficient workload distribution while meeting application deadlines. The study also highlights
trade-offs, showing that energy-efficient scheduling reduces carbon emissions with negligible impact on profitability.
In conclusion, the research provides a scalable and practical framework for cloud providers to
enhance energy sustainability while maintaining service quality. Future work includes expanding the model to support multi-core systems and variable workloads to address evolving industry
needs.

4.	Performance and Energy-Aware Bi-objective Tasks Scheduling for Cloud Data Centers
The paper addresses the rising energy consumption and environmental impact of cloud data centers driven by increasing demand for cloud services, particularly in smart cities and IoT applications. It highlights that data centers could account for up to 4.5% of global energy
consumption by 2025, necessitating energy-efficient solutions to reduce both costs and carbon emissions.
The research proposes a bi-objective optimization model for task scheduling in cloud data
centers. The model focuses on minimizing both task execution time and energy consumption by using an evolutionary algorithm. It incorporates the Locally Corrected Multiple Linear
Regression (LC-MLR) power model to accurately predict energy consumption based on resource utilization, including CPU, memory, disk, and network metrics. This contrasts with prior methods that considered only CPU and memory usage, offering a more comprehensive approach.
An intelligent autonomous agent is designed to manage task scheduling, utilizing a utility-based approach to handle conflicting objectives. Tasks are mapped to virtual machines (VMs) based on their resource requirements and service constraints, ensuring efficient workload distribution. The initial population for the evolutionary algorithm is generated using a Modified Worst Fit
Decreasing (MWFD) heuristic, which optimizes power usage by assigning tasks to the VMs where the increase in energy consumption is minimal.
The proposed model is evaluated using CloudSim, a cloud simulation tool, and compared with existing genetic algorithm-based methods. Results demonstrate significant energy savings and
reduced execution times. The GA_LC-MLR algorithm achieves lower energy consumption and a 0.5-second reduction in mean execution time compared to traditional approaches, emphasizing its effectiveness in balancing performance and energy efficiency.
The study concludes that the bi-objective model offers a scalable solution for cloud providers to optimize task scheduling while mitigating environmental impacts. Future work involves
extending the approach to dynamic and heterogeneous environments for improved adaptability.
 
5.	Energy and Network Aware Workload Management for Geographically Distributed Data Centers
The paper addresses the challenges of managing workloads in geo-distributed cloud data centers while minimizing energy and network costs. With the increasing deployment of geographically distributed data centers, cloud providers aim to leverage time-of-use (TOU) electricity pricing,
renewable energy sources, and net metering to optimize operations. However, workload
distribution across multiple data centers incurs significant network costs, queueing delays, and operational heterogeneity.
The authors propose a novel game-theoretic framework called Nash equilibrium-based
Intelligent Load Distribution (NILD), which optimizes workload assignment to minimize the combined cost of energy, network transfers, and delays. Unlike prior methods, this framework simultaneously considers factors such as data center compute capacity, cooling power, task co- location interference, and dynamic pricing models. The approach also models renewable energy utilization and net metering, allowing cloud operators to sell excess power back to the grid.
The NILD framework formulates workload distribution as a non-cooperative game where each task type independently seeks to minimize its operating cost. Tasks are allocated to data centers using an iterative heuristic that achieves Nash equilibrium, ensuring no further optimization is possible without increasing costs elsewhere.
Simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of NILD compared to traditional heuristics like Genetic Algorithm Load Distribution (GALD) and Force-Directed Load Distribution (FDLD). The framework achieves significant cost savings, reducing energy consumption by up to 54% in configurations with 16 data centers. Moreover, NILD maintains lower queueing delays and
adapts efficiently to both sinusoidal and flat workload arrival patterns.
The paper concludes that NILD provides a scalable and practical solution for cloud workload management, reducing costs and improving sustainability. Future work aims to refine the model by incorporating real-time workload variability and exploring hybrid multi-cloud environments.
 
6.	Energy-Efficient Workflow Scheduling Based on Workflow Structures Under Deadline and Budget Constraints in the Cloud
The paper addresses the critical challenge of workflow scheduling in cloud computing, focusing on energy efficiency while respecting user-defined deadlines and budgets. Workflow scheduling in the cloud is classified as an NP-complete problem due to the complexity of interdependent tasks and resource constraints. The study introduces two algorithms—Structure-based Multi- objective Workflow Scheduling with Optimal instance types (SMWSO) and Structure- based Multi-objective Workflow Scheduling with Heterogeneous instance types (SMWSH)—designed to optimize energy consumption, execution cost, and makespan.
The proposed algorithms leverage workflow structural properties to determine the optimal
number and types of virtual machines (VMs) required for task execution. SMWSO focuses on homogeneous instance types, while SMWSH incorporates heterogeneity to handle diverse
workflows. Both algorithms integrate features like Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) to minimize energy usage and employ techniques such as entry task duplication and pipeline merging to reduce execution time and idle slots.
The algorithms were evaluated using real-world scientific workflows such as Montage, SIPHT, and Epigenomics, with simulations conducted on CloudSim. Results demonstrated that SMWSO and SMWSH achieved significant energy savings (over 50% in some cases) compared to the state-of-the-art Reliability and Energy-Efficient Workflow Scheduling (REEWS) algorithm.
Additionally, SMWSO maintained a higher success rate under varying workflow sizes and workloads, proving the efficiency of its homogeneity approach.
The paper concludes that incorporating workflow structures into scheduling decisions can significantly improve energy efficiency and resource utilization. Future work aims to extend
these strategies to multi-cloud environments and address real-time workload variability using machine learning techniques.

The model considers a mix of renewable energy, grid electricity, and battery storage to address energy demands. It incorporates time-of-use electricity pricing and renewable energy availability to optimize energy procurement and usage. The framework employs Mixed-Integer Linear
Programming (MILP) to allocate workloads dynamically, accounting for factors such as battery capacity, carbon emission rates, and grid limitations. The inclusion of battery storage allows
surplus energy storage during off-peak hours and usage during peak periods, reducing reliance on grid power and mitigating the variability of renewable sources.
The model is evaluated through simulations with real-world data, demonstrating significant cost savings and carbon emission reductions. Results show the benefits of integrating renewable
energy and battery storage, including a 30-50% reduction in operational costs and lower carbon footprints. Sensitivity analysis reveals that higher renewable energy availability and larger
battery capacities enhance system performance, while flexible energy trading ("sell-back" of excess energy) further improves cost efficiency.
The research concludes that optimizing workload allocation with renewable energy integration offers a sustainable solution for edge cloud operations. Future work aims to explore dynamic scheduling with uncertainties, multi-cloud scenarios, and task deferrals to align with energy availability and demand response strategies.

9.	An Efficient Resource Utilization Technique for Scheduling Scientific Workload in Cloud Computing Environment
The paper introduces an Efficient Resource Utilization (ERU) model to optimize cache
resources for scheduling scientific workloads in heterogeneous cloud environments. The study addresses key challenges like reducing power consumption, avoiding cache failures, and meeting workload deadlines, particularly under the constraints of last-level cache (LLC) management and Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS).
The proposed ERU model employs a two-phase methodology. The first phase enhances
bandwidth usage by enabling virtual computing nodes to share cache memory, while the second phase dynamically reallocates cache resources and reconfigures virtual machines to minimize LLC failures. This dynamic partitioning and cache resizing significantly reduce energy leakage and ensure efficient resource utilization.
Unlike traditional models, the ERU approach integrates cache optimization and DVFS
dynamically rather than treating them separately. It establishes ideal relationships between cache size, core frequency, and workload execution parameters. The optimization focuses on reducing energy consumption during task execution while maintaining performance levels and minimizing execution times.
The model is validated using real-world scientific workflows, specifically SIPHT datasets. Experimental results show a 44.29% improvement in energy efficiency compared to DVFS- based scheduling methods and a 61.72% reduction in execution time compared to deadline-
constrained cost optimization hybrid cloud (DCOH) techniques. These results demonstrate the ERU model's ability to balance energy savings with improved system throughput.
In conclusion, the ERU model provides a scalable and efficient solution for managing resources in heterogeneous cloud computing frameworks. Future work will focus on applying advanced machine learning techniques and addressing diverse data-intensive workloads to enhance the model’s adaptability and performance.

Energy Management Algorithm (EMA) to optimize workflow execution time, energy consumption, and resource utilization. The study compares EMA with Shortest Job First (SJF)

The proposed EMA framework optimizes task allocation based on Makespan, prioritizing energy efficiency and resource usage. It leverages dynamic task scheduling and workload distribution to achieve lower execution times and better energy management. EMA incorporates techniques like task duplication and workload redistribution to optimize task execution on virtual machines (VMs).

DVFS. For the Montage workflow, EMA achieved a 25.73% reduction in Makespan compared to DVFS and 17.73% compared to SJF.
The experiments also show that increasing the number of VMs reduces Makespan significantly, highlighting EMA’s scalability and adaptability to larger workloads. EMA achieves these
improvements by dynamically allocating tasks to VMs with the shortest processing times and reconfiguring resources to balance workloads efficiently.
The paper concludes that EMA is an effective and energy-efficient solution for task scheduling in cloud computing, with significant potential for further optimization. Future research will focus on integrating EMA with advanced machine learning techniques and exploring its applicability to multi-cloud and hybrid environments.

12.	Deep Learning-Driven Workload Prediction and Optimization for Load Balancing in Cloud Computing Environment
The paper proposes a novel approach to workload prediction and task scheduling in cloud
computing environments using an Improved Deep Max-out model for workload forecasting combined with a Tasmanian Devil-Bald Eagle Search (TDBES) optimization algorithm for task scheduling. The study addresses the growing challenges of dynamic workload distribution, resource optimization, and energy efficiency in cloud systems.
The Deep Max-out model predicts future workloads by training on features like virtual machine (VM) capacity and task requirements, ensuring accurate predictions with minimal errors. Metrics like RMSE, MAE, and MAP show the model's superior accuracy compared to traditional methods like CNN, RNN, and Bi-GRU. Prediction results demonstrate significant improvements in workload accuracy, with RMSE values as low as 0.102 compared to 0.344 for RNN and 1.182 for Bi-GRU.
The TDBES algorithm optimizes task scheduling by balancing workloads across underloaded, equally loaded, and overloaded VMs. It combines the behavior of Tasmanian devils and bald eagles to enhance search efficiency in a meta-heuristic optimization framework. Key
performance metrics include Makespan, migration cost, and migration efficiency. TDBES achieves a migration cost of 1.55 compared to 4.987 for DBOA and a migration efficiency of 0.0989, outperforming traditional optimization methods like WACO and MPSO.
The proposed system's effectiveness is evaluated using the Google Cluster Workload Traces
dataset. Results show significant reductions in execution time, Makespan, and communication costs. For example, the execution time for a task count of 2000 is reduced to 1648 seconds, much lower than other algorithms. Additionally, TDBES demonstrates rapid convergence with the least cost at the 26th iteration during optimization.
The paper concludes that the proposed framework enhances resource utilization, improves task scheduling, and supports energy-efficient cloud operations. Future work aims to extend the
model to hybrid and multi-cloud environments while integrating real-time prediction capabilities.

distribution. The framework avoids frequent server startups and shutdowns, which consume up to 68% of the energy used at peak utilization, to optimize energy efficiency.
The EATS scheduling algorithm incorporates a linear relationship between energy consumption and task load size. It calculates energy consumption based on factors like CPU utilization, load size, and execution time, ensuring tasks are assigned to the most energy-efficient servers.
Experimental results reveal that CPU-bound tasks consume more energy than I/O-bound tasks, highlighting the importance of considering task types in scheduling decisions.
The framework was tested using benchmarks like Mencoder and AIO_Stress, demonstrating its ability to reduce energy consumption significantly. For example, CPU utilization at 100% showed an average energy consumption of 244 watts, emphasizing the need to optimize load
balancing for high energy efficiency.
The study concludes that EATS provides a scalable solution for energy-efficient cloud
computing. Future work will extend the framework to larger data center environments and compare it against other scheduling algorithms to evaluate its performance comprehensively.
 
15.	Energy-Efficient Resource Management in Virtualized Cloud Data Centers
The paper addresses the growing energy consumption in large-scale cloud data centers driven by the increasing demand for computational resources. It introduces an energy-efficient resource management system that reduces energy costs while maintaining Quality of Service (QoS) through continuous Virtual Machine (VM) consolidation and dynamic reallocation.
The proposed framework uses live migration of VMs to dynamically reallocate workloads based on real-time utilization metrics, virtual network topology, and thermal states of physical nodes.
By migrating underutilized VMs and shutting down idle servers, the system minimizes power consumption while avoiding overheating and ensuring SLA compliance.
The authors propose a decentralized architecture with local and global resource managers. Local managers monitor resource utilization and initiate VM migrations under conditions like resource overutilization, network bottlenecks, or thermal overload. Global managers oversee
migration commands and perform optimization tasks using heuristics for multi-dimensional bin- packing.
Simulation results, conducted using an enhanced version of the CloudSim toolkit, show
substantial energy savings. For example, the Minimization of Migrations (MM) policy reduced energy consumption by 83% compared to non-power-aware systems and by 66% compared to systems relying solely on Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS). The MM policy also reduced VM migrations significantly, striking a balance between energy efficiency and SLA
adherence.
The research identifies critical challenges, including balancing energy savings with performance and developing scalable algorithms for large-scale deployments. The study concludes that
dynamic VM consolidation and live migration are key to achieving sustainable cloud data centers. Future work aims to integrate network and thermal optimizations into the resource management system and deploy the framework in real-world cloud environments.

19.	Green Cloud Computing: Exploring Data Centre Energy Minimisation Strategies
The paper explores energy minimization strategies in data centers to address the rising
environmental impact of cloud computing. It highlights that while cloud computing reduces IT costs and increases operational flexibility, its energy demands contribute significantly to carbon emissions, now surpassing those of the aviation industry.

1.	Dynamic Power Management (DPM): Dynamically powers down idle servers based on workload prediction but faces challenges like prediction inaccuracies and latency.
2.	Virtualization: Allows multiple virtual machines (VMs) to share physical servers, reducing the number of active servers. However, live migration of VMs can strain network resources and increase cooling requirements.
3.	Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS): Adjusts the CPU's power and
frequency to save energy during low-demand periods. While effective, its implementation is limited by the processing delays introduced by voltage changes.
4.	Cooling System Optimization: Proposes methods like airflow management, liquid cooling, and economizers to minimize energy use in cooling infrastructures.
5.	Network Resource Management: Optimizes bandwidth allocation and uses adaptive link rates to reduce strain on network components, thereby lowering cooling
requirements.
The proposed solutions are evaluated against challenges such as balancing energy savings with Service Level Agreements (SLAs), managing workload migration, and addressing security risks in multitenant environments. Simulations and case studies indicate significant energy savings when combining these strategies, with reductions of up to 25% in power consumption.
The paper concludes that adopting a holistic approach integrating these strategies can achieve substantial energy efficiency in data centers. Future research will explore AI-driven optimization techniques and the scalability of these strategies in hybrid and multi-cloud ecosystems.

22.	Location-Aware Energy-Efficient Workload Allocation in Geo-Distributed Cloud Environment
The paper addresses energy-efficient workload allocation in geo-distributed cloud data centers to optimize energy consumption while maintaining Quality of Service (QoS). The proposed
approach considers both the execution time of tasks and the cost associated with data transfer
delays. The authors develop the Location-Aware Energy-Efficient (LAEE) workload allocation algorithm using a meta-heuristic genetic algorithm to solve the non-linear optimization problem.
The key objectives of the algorithm are:
1.	Minimizing Makespan: Reducing the total execution time of data-intensive workloads by considering task allocation and data transfer times.
2.	Energy Efficiency: Leveraging Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) to adjust server power usage based on workload demands.
3.	Delay Awareness: Incorporating network delays into the allocation process to optimize the placement of tasks near their associated data.
Simulation results using CloudSim demonstrate that the LAEE algorithm significantly
outperforms traditional methods such as Round Robin (RR), Shortest Job First (SJF), and other genetic-based algorithms in terms of makespan reduction and energy savings. The proposed method achieves:
•	15%-28% improvement in workload makespan compared to RR and SJF.
•	Up to 8% reduction in energy consumption when integrated with DVFS, demonstrating its potential for green computing.
The paper concludes that the LAEE algorithm effectively balances energy efficiency and task execution times while reducing SLA violations. Future research aims to expand its applicability to real-time scenarios and explore hybrid multi-cloud environments.
 
23.	Optimized Load-Balancing in Cloud Computing Based on Traffic and Workload-Aware VM Migration
The paper introduces an Osmotic Hybrid Bee and Ant Colony with Future Utilization Prediction (OH-BAC-FUP) approach to improve load balancing and energy efficiency in cloud computing environments. The method addresses the challenges of managing traffic congestion and workload distribution across heterogeneous physical machines (PMs) and virtual machines (VMs).
The OH-BAC-FUP approach leverages Linear Regression (LR) and Optimal Piecewise Linear Regression (OPLR) to predict current and future resource utilization, including CPU, memory,
bandwidth, and storage. This prediction helps identify optimal PMs for VM migration to balance loads effectively. The system integrates a Multipath Traffic Routing (MTR) protocol to avoid network congestion by splitting traffic across multiple disjoint paths.
To enhance energy efficiency, the Merge-and-Split-based Coalitional Game-theoretic (MSCG) scheme is introduced. This method partitions PMs into coalitions based on their
workload levels and migrates VMs to minimize energy usage. The combined OH-BAC-FUP- MTR-MSCG approach ensures high energy efficiency by dynamically shutting down idle PMs while maintaining task reliability.
Simulation results using the CloudSim API demonstrate the system's effectiveness. Key findings include:
•	A 39.5% reduction in energy consumption compared to classical VM migration techniques.
•	Significant improvements in SLA metrics, with reductions in SLA violations (SLAV) and SLA time achieved (SLATAH).
•	A 74.6% decrease in performance degradation due to VM migration (PDM).
•	An 85% reduction in VM migrations and fewer PM shutdowns, leading to enhanced system efficiency.
The paper concludes that the OH-BAC-FUP-MTR-MSCG approach outperforms existing methods in load balancing, energy savings, and traffic management. Future work will explore
further refinements, including real-time adaptability and broader applications across multi-cloud systems.
 
24.	Quality and Energy Optimized Scheduling Technique for Executing Scientific Workload in Cloud Computing Environment
The paper introduces the Quality and Energy Optimized Scheduling (QEOS) technique to address the challenges of executing data-intensive scientific workflows in heterogeneous cloud
computing environments. Traditional workload scheduling models face limitations, such as high interaction costs and poor resource utilization, which lead to inefficiencies in energy
consumption and processing time.
QEOS leverages Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS) to minimize energy usage by dynamically adjusting CPU frequencies based on workload demands. The technique also
optimizes task scheduling by balancing processing efficiency and resource usage, ensuring
compliance with Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and Quality of Service (QoS) requirements. Key contributions include:
1.	Efficient workload scheduling that reduces both energy consumption and processing time for heterogeneous cloud environments.
2.	Incorporation of DVFS and optimized interaction cost models to enhance system performance without increasing execution overhead.
3.	A multi-core framework that adapts dynamically to task demands, ensuring scalability and reduced turnaround times.
Experimental evaluations were conducted using the CloudSim simulator and the Inspiral scientific workflow. Results show that QEOS significantly outperforms existing DVFS-based scheduling models. Specifically:
•	Processing time is reduced by 87.82% on average compared to traditional methods.
•	Average execution time improves by 91.46%, demonstrating its scalability for large workloads.
•	Energy consumption decreases by 19.45%, achieving a favorable trade-off between system performance and energy efficiency.
The study concludes that QEOS provides an effective solution for managing scientific workloads in cloud environments. Future work will focus on improving resource utilization and reducing
I/O overhead through enhanced cloud resource management.
 
25.	Workload Allocation Toward Energy Consumption-Delay Trade-Off in Cloud-Fog Computing Using Multi-Objective NPSO Algorithm
The paper presents a Multi-Objective Non-Dominated Sorting Particle Swarm Optimization (NPSO) algorithm for workload allocation in cloud-fog computing environments. The study aims to address the trade-off between energy consumption and task delay, which are critical
challenges for distributed computing systems supporting latency-sensitive applications like IoT and real-time data analytics.
Key contributions of the algorithm include:
1.	Energy-Delay Trade-Off:
o	The NPSO algorithm optimizes resource allocation to balance energy efficiency with task execution delays.
o	Incorporates objectives such as minimizing energy consumption and reducing task completion time to meet service requirements.
2.	Fog-Cloud Collaboration:
o	Integrates fog nodes for local processing to reduce latency and bandwidth usage, while leveraging cloud resources for computationally intensive tasks.
3.	Dynamic Workload Management:
o	Utilizes predictive modeling to dynamically allocate workloads based on resource availability and workload characteristics.
o	Ensures scalability and adaptability to varying workloads and user demands.
Simulation results show that the proposed algorithm significantly outperforms traditional methods such as Round Robin (RR) and First Come First Serve (FCFS). Specific findings include:
•	A 28% reduction in energy consumption compared to baseline algorithms.
•	A 32% improvement in task completion time, highlighting the efficiency of the fog- cloud collaboration.
•	Enhanced resource utilization and reduced SLA violations.
The study concludes that the NPSO algorithm provides an effective solution for optimizing workload allocation in cloud-fog systems, ensuring energy efficiency and low latency. Future
research will focus on integrating real-time data streams and further refining resource allocation strategies for hybrid cloud-fog environments.
